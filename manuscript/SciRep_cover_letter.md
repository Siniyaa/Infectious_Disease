The Editorial Board

Dear Editors,

We submit for your consideration our manuscript, **"Threshold and time-window choices determine whether
nirsevimab escape substitutions appear to have increased in public respiratory syncytial virus genomes"**,
for publication as an Article in *Scientific Reports*.

Nirsevimab and clesrovimab are now in widespread use for infant prophylaxis against respiratory syncytial
virus, and several groups have reported the frequency of resistance-associated substitutions in the fusion
(F) protein since rollout. Those reports reach different conclusions. We asked how much of the disagreement
is attributable to the virus and how much to choices the analyst makes and rarely reports.

We assembled the combined public sequence archive - 25,384 GenBank and 44,275 eligible GISAID EpiRSV
records, 38,925 after de-duplication across both databases - processed both sources uniformly, called F
variants against each subtype's own consensus rather than a single reference, and classified them using
published deep mutational scanning scores. Three results follow.

First, no score-based rule applied to one substitution at a time recovers the substitutions actually
reported as resistance-associated. Five of seventeen meet the criteria; three fail the cell-entry filter at
any Fab-escape cutoff, including one member of a pair whose resistance was confirmed by fusion-inhibition
assay; and capturing F:K68Q, which has a published clinical case, requires a cutoff at which at least 4.5%
of all archive records would be classified as carriers.

Second, whether escape substitutions appear to have increased after rollout depends on the collection-year
window. Within a 2017-onward window the contrast is strong - no carrier before 1 July 2023 against 35 of
26,064 after (0.134%, 95% CI 0.094-0.187; Fisher p = 1.3 × 10⁻⁵) - but across the full archive the same
comparison is not significant (p = 0.44), because the same substitutions are present in deposits from 2002
and 2014-15. Third, it also depends on where the threshold is set, losing significance at intermediate
cutoffs and reversing at permissive ones.

We believe the work suits *Scientific Reports* specifically. Its central result is a methodological caution
built on a partly negative finding, and such results are judged poorly against perceived importance but
well against technical soundness, which is the criterion your journal applies. The paper does not claim that
previous reports are wrong. Our full-archive result is concordant with the prior archive-scale analysis of
the binding site by Wilkins and colleagues; what is new is that the same data, restricted to a modern
window, support the opposite conclusion. The practical recommendation that follows - that reported
resistance frequencies be accompanied by the classification rule, the reference baseline and the
collection-year span - is directly usable by the surveillance groups producing these estimates.

The analysis is reproducible from public data. Accession manifests for every analysis population, the
complete de-duplication group mapping, the maximum-likelihood and temporally rooted phylogenies, the
TreeTime output, every derived table and the analysis workflow are deposited at
https://github.com/Siniyaa/Infectious_Disease. Sequence data are not redistributed, in accordance with
GISAID terms; the manifests allow any user with GISAID access to reconstitute the cohort exactly.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. All
authors have approved the submission. The study used only existing public sequence deposits, with no human
participants, no clinical specimens and no identifiable individual data, so ethical approval was not
required. As disclosed in full in the manuscript, a generative artificial-intelligence assistant was used
substantially in writing and debugging the analysis code, running the analyses and drafting and revising
the text; the authors defined the research question, directed the analysis, reviewed the code and outputs,
verified every reference against its primary source, and take full responsibility for the content and the
conclusions. Competing interests are declared in the manuscript.
